What we're for

Most operating knowledge lives in someone's head.

Every organization runs on processes nobody has written down and decisions nobody has time to research. The person who knows how billing really works is the person who is too busy doing billing to explain it. The question that would settle an argument never gets asked because fielding it takes three weeks.

That knowledge gap is not a documentation problem. It's a speed problem. By the time the process map is drawn by hand or the survey comes back from a research firm, the thing you needed it for has already been decided badly.

Our products close that gap by making the expensive part — the extraction, the structuring, the analysis — fast enough to be worth doing at all.

How we work

Four commitments we hold ourselves to.

Products before hours

We sell software, not headcount. Consulting exists to sharpen the products, not to fill a utilization target — so engagements are deliberately limited and always product-led.

Evidence stays attached

Every output our software generates traces back to its source: the conversation it came from, the responses behind it, the step in the map it describes. An assertion you cannot audit is an assertion we do not ship.

Built in the open workflow

We use our own tools daily before anyone else sees them. The process mapping ran on our own operations first; the review discipline we sell is the one our commits go through.

Honest about limits

AI systems fail in specific, knowable ways. We would rather tell you where a model is unreliable and what we do about it than let you discover it in front of your team.

What working with us looks like

Small, fast, and pointed at one real problem.

01

Start with the thing that's stuck

Not a transformation program. One process nobody can explain, or one decision that keeps getting deferred. Narrow enough that you know within weeks whether it worked.

02

Put the software in the middle of it

We deploy the product against your real work — real conversations, real people, real messiness. Demos on clean data teach you nothing about whether a system holds up.

03

Keep what earns its place

If the output isn't good enough to use, we say so and fix it or stop. The measure is whether your team reaches for it next month without being asked.

Tell us what's stuck.

A short conversation is enough to know whether we can help — and we'll tell you plainly if we can't.
