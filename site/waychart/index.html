The problem

Process documentation is always out of date, because writing it is somebody's least favorite job.

The people who know how a process actually runs are the people running it. Getting that knowledge out of their heads has traditionally meant workshops, whiteboards, and a consultant with a template — a week of everyone's time to produce a diagram that's wrong by the next quarter.

So most organizations don't have it. They have a folder of stale SOPs nobody opens, and a set of undocumented workarounds that are the real process. When someone leaves, the workaround leaves with them.

Waychart makes the extraction cheap enough to redo whenever reality changes. One conversation, and the map exists.

What it does

From one conversation to the documents your team actually uses.

Map

AI-drawn process maps from a single recorded conversation — swimlanes, decisions, and handoffs included.

Document

Reports and step-by-step SOPs generated from the map itself, branded and ready to circulate.

Benchmark

The same process compared across your own sites — see who does it leaner and where the time goes.

Review

Structured review loops route open questions to the people who know, and fold answers back into the map.

Estimate

Time and cost estimates per step, so a map reads as an operating budget, not just a diagram.

Reconcile

Upload the official SOP and see where reality disagrees — a conformance-gap register, automatically.

Being straight with you

What Waychart doesn't do.

It doesn't replace the person who knows the process — it interviews them faster. The first map is a draft, and the review step exists because the draft will have gaps only your team can close.

It doesn't run your operation. It produces maps, documents, and comparisons; acting on them is still a management decision.

And it works best on processes people can describe out loud. Anything that lives entirely inside a system's logs is a different problem.
